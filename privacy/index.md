# Allegedly privacy policy

Last updated: 22 October 2026

Allegedly is a media player for Plex, made by Branden Wolf (Wolf Software
Studio). This page explains what the app does with your information. The
short version: **Allegedly has no servers of its own, shows no ads, and
collects nothing about you for itself.** It talks only to Plex and to
services you choose to connect it to.

## What the app keeps on your device

- **Your Plex sign-in.** When you sign in with Plex, the sign-in token Plex
  gives the app is kept in your device's secure storage (Android Keystore
  or Windows Credential Manager) so you stay signed in. It is never shown,
  logged or sent anywhere except to Plex and to the services listed below.
- **Your settings.** Home screen layout, pinned libraries, audio and
  subtitle preferences, profiles and profile locks are stored on your
  device only. Profile PINs and direction codes are stored as one-way
  hashes, not as the PIN itself.
- **Addresses and keys you enter** for optional services (below), in the
  same secure storage.

Uninstalling the app, or signing out, removes these from your device.

## Who the app talks to

- **Plex** (plex.tv and the Plex Media Servers your account can reach), to
  sign you in, list servers and libraries, show artwork and play media.
  This is the same information any Plex app uses. Plex's own privacy
  policy applies to Plex: https://www.plex.tv/about/privacy-legal/
- **Optional services you set up yourself**, only if you enter their
  address in Settings → Integrations:
  - **AskFirst**, a family app where children ask a parent before watching
    a title. The app sends who is asking and for which title.
  - **Overseerr**, to discover and request movies and series. The app signs
    in to your Overseerr with your Plex sign-in, so it can show and make
    requests for you.
  These run on servers chosen by the owner of the Plex server, not by the
  makers of Allegedly.
- **The Movie Database (TMDB) image servers**, to show posters and
  backdrops on Discover pages, when Overseerr is set up.
- **YouTube**, only when you choose to watch a trailer; it opens in the
  YouTube app or your browser.

## What the app does not do

- No advertising, no analytics, no tracking, no crash reporting to us.
- No selling or sharing of your information.
- No account with us: you sign in with your existing Plex account.
- It does not access your contacts, location, camera, microphone, photos
  or other files.

## Watching and playback

Like every Plex app, Allegedly tells your Plex server what you are watching
and how far you got, so you can resume later and see what you have
watched. That information stays between the app and the Plex servers you
use.

## Children

Allegedly is not directed at children. It includes parental controls
(profile locks, and the optional AskFirst service) so that parents can
decide what children in their household can watch.

## Security

Connections to Plex and to online services use encryption (HTTPS).
Servers on your own home network may be reached without it if the server's
owner set them up that way.

## Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date.
